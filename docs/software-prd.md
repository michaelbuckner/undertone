**Software PRD — generative accompaniment synth**

Draft v0.6 · October 31, 2026 · Proposed requirements unless explicitly marked confirmed; updated after Mike's Ghosts-inspired composition feedback

**Product purpose.** Give Mike an enjoyable harmonic backing part within a minute, keep it dependable while he plays bass, and let it evolve when he wants new ideas. It should also provide pads and rhythmic chord textures for electronic composition alongside his Elektron instruments.

The primary user is a bass player who enjoys beat creation and wants to explore building an instrument. Confirmed gear: Digitone II, Digitakt I, and Syntakt. Confirmed musical interests: ambient, neo-soul, funk, and the slower dark ambient side of later Nine Inch Nails. These inform sound and behavior presets; they do not imply recreating specific songs or recordings. Mike has an Apple silicon Mac available for the Mac validation pass.

**What success feels like.** Open the app, choose a key and a starting character, press Generate and Play, and start playing bass. A useful progression can remain unchanged for an entire practice session. Turning up variation introduces controlled changes without losing the key, phrase length, or beat. The instrument leaves space for the bass and drums.

**Scope and assumptions.** The MVP is a native standalone application with its own sound engine, operating offline without accounts or cloud generation. Generative means musical rules plus repeatable randomness. Machine learning is not required. One harmonic progression drives a synth bed and optional broken-chord/melody roles. These are accompaniment controls, not a general multitrack workstation. Audio and MIDI settings are separate from the main musical controls.

Windows and macOS are confirmed requirements. Proposed initial release matrix: Windows 11 x64; macOS 14 or later on Apple silicon and Intel. Confirm Mike's actual systems and the selected framework's minimum versions before freezing this matrix. Windows ARM, Linux desktop distribution, and mobile are outside the initial release. A Windows build alone does not satisfy the MVP.

Priority labels: P0 is required for the MVP; P1 follows a successful MVP; Future is exploratory.

**Core playing journeys.**

1. Solo practice: choose D Dorian and a warm neo-soul preset, generate four chords, enable Bass Space, press Play, and lock a phrase worth practicing. The app plays the harmony while Mike supplies the bass.
2. Elektron jam: connect one Elektron clock source, choose its MIDI input in the app, and start playback from that device. Beats and chords follow the same tempo. Hear both through a suitable mixer or audio interface.
3. Ambient exploration: select slow changes and long envelopes, enable Evolve with low Variation, and lock two anchor chords. Save an interesting result as a session.
4. Dark ambient composition: choose a sustained minor progression and a slow evolving tone, then lock the harmony. Use texture, restrained drive and space to introduce movement while the Elektron instruments provide the drum arrangement. Detailed song arrangement remains external; the v0.8 revision adds output recording and generated MIDI export.

**Generation contract.** Begin with weighted progression templates and scale-derived substitutions, not unrestricted random note selection. Favor a tonal center, include deliberate repetition, and preserve a coherent return from the last chord to the first. Treat voice leading as choosing notes that move short distances between chords; avoid repeated leaps or dense low-register clusters. Do not label results as guaranteed to be musically pleasing.

Complexity selects allowed chord vocabulary and maximum note count. A ninth must belong to the selected pitch collection; unavailable extensions are omitted rather than silently changing the mode. Root omission keeps the displayed chord identity and removes its root from the sounding voicing, leaving room for the bassist. If a requested voicing cannot fit the register, remove optional extensions first, then widen upward within the allowed range; never place notes below the configured floor.

In Loop mode, every cycle repeats the stored note events exactly. Evolve has independent chord and tone targets. At each selected bar boundary, positive Variation changes at least one unlocked chord to a different scale degree, favoring the chord that will sound at that boundary. Additional changes depend on Variation. Keep key, meter, slot duration, register and locked voicings unchanged. Tone movement is gradual and independent of chord locks; Variation controls its depth. Zero disables both targets. Evolve controls take effect immediately. Manual queued harmonic edits take priority until their phrase boundary. Provide visible feedback when no change is possible. See [the v0.2 behavior contract](sound-design-0.2.md) for precise timing and recall semantics.

Generate queues a new phrase when playing and applies immediately when stopped. Key/mode, phrase length, register, and rhythm changes also queue to a phrase boundary; continuous timbre controls are smoothed in real time. If a key or register change conflicts with a locked voicing, show the conflict and require an explicit unlock before applying that change. Lock All immediately cancels queued harmonic replacements. Undo restores the previous phrase at the next boundary, or immediately while stopped.

Use a seeded random generator and a versioned event model. Identical saved state and identical control actions at the same boundaries must reproduce the same note-event sequence on both platforms. This does not require bit-identical audio rendering. Saving stores the currently realized phrase plus the next evolution state; loading starts stopped at the beginning of that phrase, not mid-note. Session files retain realized notes even if a future generator version cannot reproduce the original algorithm.

**Sound and interface.** Retain the original warm engine and add original architectures inspired by a three-oscillator ladder-filter synth, DCO/pulse/chorus synth, and four-operator FM synth. These are distinct DSP paths, not renamed macro settings or claims of exact hardware emulation. Sound selection is independent of musical character. The shared effects flow is voice → drive → chorus → reverb → output high-pass → output protection. New engines expose attack/decay/sustain/release, texture and supported filter controls. Smooth engine/parameter changes and voice stealing; excessive release tails must not accumulate without bound. Architecture references and implementation limits are in [sound design](sound-design-0.2.md).

The main screen has a transport/status row, a visible chord strip, harmony controls, and sound macros. Primary controls are Key/Mode, Character, Complexity, Variation, Bass Space, Brightness, Motion, Space, and Level. Character loads a clearly labeled starting preset; it must not silently replace a playing phrase. Advanced controls expose register, root omission, articulation, envelopes, drive, and separate effect amounts. MIDI/audio routing lives in settings. The interface uses the same concepts later mapped to physical controls.

Preset directions are testable starting briefs: ambient uses long attacks and slow harmony; neo-soul uses sevenths/ninths and smooth voicings; funk uses short syncopated articulation with decaying filter envelopes; dark ambient uses sustained minor harmony, slow evolving texture, restrained drive and long tails, informed by Mike's preference for later dark ambient Nine Inch Nails. Avoid automatically generating bass lines. Mike subsequently requested optional glitchy percussion informed by later Radiohead; keep it easy to mute and mix quietly alongside the existing rig, using original synthesized sounds and patterns.

**Elektron synchronization.** The default topology is one of Mike's Elektron devices as clock leader, with the app following. The manuals for [Digitone II](https://www.elektron.se/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Digitone-2-User-Manual_ENG_OS1.00A_241023.pdf), [Digitakt I](https://www.elektron.se/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Digitakt_User_Manual_ENG_OS1.51_231108.pdf), and [Syntakt](https://www.elektron.se/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Syntakt-User-Manual_ENG_OS1.30B_250129.pdf) document clock and transport configuration. These references establish protocol support, not tested compatibility with this proposed app or the user's installed firmware.

Support MIDI via the operating system's exposed USB MIDI ports or a USB-to-DIN MIDI interface. MIDI carries timing/control data; audio from the app still needs an audio output. An interface or mixer can combine it with Elektron audio and the bass. No device-specific audio routing, Overbridge dependency, or USB audio support from a particular box is assumed.

The implementation receives the standard 24 clock ticks per quarter note and distinct Start/Continue/Stop messages described by the [MIDI Association](https://midi.org/summary-of-midi-1-0-messages). Start resets the phrase to its beginning; Stop retains position and releases notes; Continue resumes that position with the chord re-articulated. Clock ticks alone do not auto-start playback. External mode disables local tempo editing. Switching clock source while playing stops cleanly and waits for a fresh Start.

After 500 ms without expected clock ticks during external playback, stop scheduling notes, release voices, and show Clock Lost. Do not fall back silently to internal tempo. Reconnection waits for a new Start. Receiving regular ticks after a local application launch establishes tempo but cannot recover song position by itself. Song Position Pointer, automatic Elektron pattern changes, and arbitrary mid-song alignment are deferred. The MVP shares tempo and start position, not pattern identities. Filter unrelated MIDI input and do not echo incoming clock.

**Engineering proposal.** Use C++ with JUCE for desktop audio, MIDI, and UI, subject to a small cross-platform feasibility build and review of distribution/licensing requirements. JUCE documents native Windows, macOS, and Linux applications from one codebase, making it a plausible route toward a later small-computer prototype. See [JUCE features](https://juce.com/features/). This is a proposed implementation choice, not a user requirement.

Keep harmony/voicing, deterministic generation, event scheduling, synthesis, and preset serialization in independently testable modules. The harmony core must not depend on desktop UI or JUCE device objects. An embedded board may reuse that core while requiring new audio drivers, effects, and UI; full desktop portability is not assumed.

Schedule notes using the audio timeline, not UI timers. File access, generation work that may allocate, and device discovery stay off the audio callback. Use bounded event queues and smoothed parameters. Proposed audio backends are WASAPI on Windows with ASIO where available and appropriately supported, and Core Audio on macOS. Validate the actual interface/driver combination before promising latency.

**MVP acceptance and release gates.** All numbers below are targets to measure, not performance claims. Record test machine, OS, interface, driver, sample rate, and buffer size. Select at least one modest Windows reference machine and one Mac before the performance gate; test Intel Mac explicitly if it remains in the supported matrix.

| Gate | Acceptance evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Musical usefulness | Mike completes a 20-minute bass jam with each of the ambient, neo-soul, and funk starting styles, rates at least one preset in each 4/5 or better for usefulness, and does not need to change key/range settings to fix unexpected low notes. Dark ambient presets are evaluated separately as composition textures. |
| Setup | After initial device configuration, Mike reaches audible accompaniment within 60 seconds in three consecutive launches. |
| Harmony correctness | Automated tests across all roots, modes, and complexity levels confirm allowed pitches, note floors, note counts, lock preservation, and matching saved/reloaded note events. A fixed seed corpus produces identical events on Windows and Mac. |
| Playback stability | On each reference machine, 60 minutes at 48 kHz/256 samples with 16 voices and effects produces no app-attributable underruns, crashes, stuck notes, or non-finite output. Assess actual output recordings for clicks. |
| Timing | Internal scheduled events land within one audio sample of their intended sample positions. For each supported external-clock route, use a sharp test patch and loopback recording: after acquisition and fixed-offset calibration, 95% of chord onsets are within 10 ms of the reference grid, with no accumulated bar slip over 20 minutes at 60, 120, and 180 BPM. Log failures by route; do not hide them behind a global compatibility claim. |
| Device behavior | Test each Elektron as leader on Windows and Mac using USB MIDI, plus a representative DIN interface route on both OS families. Test Start/Stop/Continue, 120→90→140 BPM changes, cable removal, clock loss, audio-device removal, and clean recovery. Record firmware and routing. |
| Headroom | At the worst supported patch/voice setting, audio processing uses less than 70% of its callback time at the 99th percentile on the reference machines. If this fails, simplify effects or revise the supported baseline explicitly. |
| Session durability | Save, close, and reopen reproduce the phrase and settings. Invalid or unsupported files give a useful error without overwriting them. An interrupted save preserves the previous valid copy; recovery opens stopped. |
| Delivery | Installable Windows and macOS builds pass clean-machine launch and offline playback checks. Signing/notarization is handled before public distribution; personal test builds document any OS trust prompts. |

**Delivery sequence.**

1. Feasibility: native audio and a fixed four-chord loop on Windows and Mac; prove build/distribution tooling and one usable patch.
2. Musical prototype: scale-aware generation, voicing, Bass Space, locks, undo, and session recall. Mike evaluates whether the musical foundation is worth continuing.
3. Full MVP: Evolve, eight presets, articulation, effects, MIDI follow, recovery, and the acceptance matrix above.
4. After the current Windows refinements: prioritize validation and musical listening, then external MIDI note output, controller mapping and hardware experiments. Generated MIDI export and live WAV capture are included in v0.8. Plug-in work is separate scope.

**Open decisions.** Confirm the actual Windows/Mac models, audio interface and mixer route, preferred clock leader, comfortable complexity of chord names, and whether industrial creation makes MIDI/WAV export more urgent than hardware. These do not block this planning draft. They should be resolved before freezing the implementation backlog. Budget and schedule remain unestimated until the feasibility build and available development time are known.
